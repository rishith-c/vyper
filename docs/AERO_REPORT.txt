=== arms ===
two blades, 9 x 18 mm, 255 mm long, crossing at 77 deg
blade frontal silhouette      34.8 cm^2
  The blades make no useful lift and are not meant to. The whole
  point of this layout is that a 9 mm blade edge-on costs almost
  nothing, so the drag budget is body + motors and nothing else.

=== frame geometry ===
stretched X: 176 mm fore-aft / 140 mm lateral = 1.26:1
lateral spacing sits at the 127 mm prop minimum + 13 mm
exposed blade frontal area  34.8 cm^2

=== drag build-up ===
  fuselage           CdA =   3.02 cm^2  (   8 %)
  2 blades           CdA =   6.96 cm^2  (  18 %)
  4 motor bells      CdA =  28.67 cm^2  (  74 %)
  interference x1.15
  TOTAL              CdA =  44.45 cm^2
  open racer, same class   75.00 cm^2   -> 41 % less drag
  NOTE: the motors are now the single largest drag item. Fairing
  the airframe has made the bare bells the thing worth attacking.

=== top speed ===
prop 5x4.3 at ~30,303 rpm -> pitch speed  55.2 m/s
realistic max level speed       41.4 m/s =   149 km/h
drag there  4.66 N against  62.8 N available
  Thrust is NOT the limit -- prop pitch is. This is the single most
  important result here: the fairing does not raise the ceiling,
  it lowers what it costs to sit at the ceiling.

=== what the fairing actually buys ===
  at 30 m/s (108 km/h): drag 2.45 N vs 4.13 N open  ->  41 % less power to hold speed
  at 40 m/s (144 km/h): drag 4.36 N vs 7.35 N open  ->  41 % less power to hold speed

=== hover ===
disc area (4 props)           507 cm^2
disc loading                118.6 N/m^2
induced velocity              7.0 m/s
ideal hover power              42 W  (x ~1.9 for real figure of merit -> 79 W)
blade area inside the discs    42 cm^2 =  8.2 % of disc area  (measured)
estimated hover download    0.5 % of thrust  =   31 g
  Two thin blades block far less of the disc than the wing panels
  did, which is the other half of why this layout is better here.
